# Thermal Dynamics of Peri-Urban Landscapes: A Paired Spatial Analysis of Brick Kilns and Bare Soil in León, Guanajuato

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.21330162.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21330162)


<img src="public/logo.png" alt="Logo de Evaluasaurio" width="100%"/>

## Overview
This repository contains the spatial extraction scripts, statistical analysis notebooks, geospatial datasets, and generated figures for the study examining the thermal behavior of artisanal brick kilns in León, Guanajuato, Mexico. The research utilizes a paired multi-buffer spatial approach to distinguish the thermal contribution of kiln activity from the surrounding bare soil landscape. Landsat 8 Collection 2 Level-2 imagery acquired during the dry seasons (March–May) of 2024–2026 was processed using Google Earth Engine.

## Highlights
* Paired design isolates thermal impacts of peri-urban soil exposure.
* Bare soil, not brick kilns, is the main driver of LST anomalies.
* LST-NDMI coupling confirms severe water stress in peri-urban zones.
* 3-year Landsat analysis provides a robust baseline for León, Mexico.
* Multi-buffer method reveals thermal dissipation limits at 500 m.

## Key Findings
Statistical analysis utilizing Wilcoxon signed-rank and Mann-Whitney U tests indicates that bare-soil control sites consistently exhibit higher Land Surface Temperature (LST) values than active brick kilns. The mean thermal difference observed was approximately $\Delta LST \approx -3.7^{\circ}C$. Furthermore, a moderate inverse relationship exists between LST and NDMI ($\rho \approx -0.490$, p < 0.001), confirming that higher surface temperatures are strictly associated with reduced vegetation moisture. These results suggest that exposed soil is the dominant factor explaining the observed thermal anomalies, exceeding the direct thermal contribution of the brick kilns.

## Open Data Availability (Zenodo)
The twelve CSV files generated by Google Earth Engine, including pixel-level data, polygon statistics, buffer statistics, and paired tables for 2024, 2025, and 2026, are publicly available on Zenodo.
